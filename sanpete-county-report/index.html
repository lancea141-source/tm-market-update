What this means for a seller here. Sanpete carries 8.2 months of inventory against 2.9 on the Wasatch Front, and homes take a median of 57 days to sell rather than 31. Buyers here have time and choice, and sellers are negotiating — the sale-to-list ratio is 98.6%. In a market like this the opening price does most of the work: there is no wave of competing offers to correct an optimistic number, so a listing that starts too high tends to sit rather than get bid up.

Part 1b — Manufactured Housing

Manufactured homes trade in three legally distinct ways and do not price alike. Pooling them produces a misleading figure, so each is reported separately.

TypeSold CompsMedian Structure $/SqFt
Manufactured — owned land, real property0—
Manufactured — owned land, personal property (title not surrendered)0—
Manufactured — park / leased lot (chattel)0—
Manufactured — owned land, real property. Affixed to a permanent foundation with the vehicle title surrendered. Sells as real estate; conventional and FHA financing available.
Manufactured — owned land, personal property (title not surrendered). The home is still legally titled as a vehicle. Buyers cannot use a standard mortgage — chattel financing or cash only, which narrows the buyer pool and pulls value down. Curable before listing via an affidavit of affixture.
Manufactured — park / leased lot (chattel). No land conveys. Values as personal property, depreciates on a vehicle-like curve, and lot rent caps resale. Land comps do not apply.

Model fit note. The regression for this band explains 41% of price variation — rural land carries wide differences in access, water rights and topography that MLS fields do not capture. The median price per acre is used for valuation rather than the regression, as a median is unaffected by that scatter.
Why two bands. Price per acre is not linear — a 5-acre parcel prices far higher per acre than a 40-acre one. The wide band describes the market; the tight band is the only defensible basis for valuing a specific parcel.

Why the middle method. Vacant land trades at $22,518 an acre, but a buyer purchasing an improved property does not pay full dirt price for every surrounding acre — the residential model puts the contributory value of an additional acre at $16,237. Method A overstates the land, Method C understates the homesite the house actually sits on. Valuing the homesite at land value and the remaining acreage at its contributory rate is the approach an appraiser would defend.
